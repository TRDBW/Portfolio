Prioritisation within a Sprint

8 Jun 2026 · 6 min read

Why the Sprint Goal matters more than mechanically taking the next ticket

Product Backlog ordering and Sprint Backlog management are related, but they are not the same activity.

The Product Backlog communicates the broader order in which potential work should be considered.

The Sprint Backlog is the Developers' current plan for achieving the Sprint Goal.

Once a Sprint begins, Developers should not be expected to take the next ticket from the top of a queue without considering technical sequence, collaboration or the goal of the Sprint.

Product Backlog vs Sprint Backlog

Interactive — select a side

Two related plans, owned differently and used differently. Neither is a frozen subset of the other.

Begin with the Sprint Goal

The Sprint Goal describes the outcome the team intends to achieve.

It gives the Sprint a purpose beyond completing a collection of unrelated tickets.

Suppose a Sprint Goal is:

Enable a small group of customers to complete an online card payment successfully.

The Sprint Backlog might include:

  • Payment-provider authentication
  • The basic payment journey
  • Transaction recording
  • Essential monitoring
  • Focused testing

The Developers may decide to work on authentication and the payment journey together while another team member prepares test scenarios.

That may be more effective than assigning each person the next independent ticket.

The question within the Sprint is:

What is the best next action to achieve the Sprint Goal?

Technical sequencing

Some work has a necessary technical order.

A team may need to:

  • Agree an interface before integrating components
  • Create test data before running a scenario
  • Complete a security change before releasing
  • Establish monitoring before exposing a risky capability
  • Resolve a defect before continuing related development

Technical sequence should be visible, but it should not become a rigid mini-waterfall.

Teams can often:

  • Work against mocks
  • Agree contracts early
  • Pair across components
  • Build thin end-to-end slices
  • Test incrementally
  • Progress dependent work in parallel

The Sprint Backlog should reflect the current plan, not an unchangeable sequence created at Sprint Planning.

Collaboration and swarming

Starting more items does not necessarily produce more progress.

When every Developer takes a separate ticket, the team can create:

  • High work in progress
  • Delayed testing
  • Integration problems
  • Partially completed work
  • Slow feedback

Swarming means several team members collaborate to complete an important item or remove a blocker.

This can include:

  • Pairing on a complex change
  • Developers helping with automated testing
  • Testers contributing during implementation
  • Specialists reviewing an approach early
  • Team members jointly investigating an incident

The aim is not to place the entire team on every ticket.

It is to optimise the flow of completed outcomes rather than individual utilisation.

New information during delivery

A Sprint is not protected from reality.

During delivery, the team may discover that:

  • A proposed solution is unnecessary
  • An integration behaves differently from its documentation
  • A dependency is unavailable
  • A defect makes the planned approach unsafe
  • A smaller outcome can satisfy the Sprint Goal
  • Part of the planned scope has little value

The Sprint Backlog should be updated as the team learns.

Change is not automatically a failure of planning. Ignoring evidence to preserve the original ticket list is not agility.

Urgent production incidents

Some incidents justify interrupting a Sprint.

Others can be assessed, recorded and scheduled without displacing the current goal.

Teams need agreed criteria based on:

  • Number of affected users
  • Severity
  • Financial impact
  • Security impact
  • Availability of a workaround
  • Operational burden
  • Reputational impact
  • Regulatory consequences

When an incident must enter the Sprint, the impact should be discussed rather than hidden.

The Product Owner and Developers may:

  • Remove lower-value scope
  • Reduce the planned outcome
  • Replace an item
  • Create a focused incident response
  • Reconsider whether the Sprint Goal remains achievable

Adding urgent work without removing or renegotiating anything creates an unrealistic plan.

Protect focus without becoming inflexible

A team should not accept every new request during a Sprint.

Constant change creates:

  • Lost focus
  • Unfinished work
  • Reduced predictability
  • More defects
  • Frustrated team members
  • Unclear accountability

However, protecting the Sprint does not mean refusing to consider significant new evidence.

A useful response is:

  1. Assess the new information.
  2. Determine whether it threatens the Sprint Goal.
  3. Understand the consequence of delay.
  4. Discuss the impact with the Product Owner.
  5. Renegotiate scope where necessary.
  6. Communicate the decision.

This protects focus while allowing responsible adaptation.

Avoid ticket-by-ticket utilisation

A team is not successful because every person is busy.

A team is successful when valuable, usable work is completed.

Developers may sometimes spend time:

  • Helping another team member
  • Reviewing an approach
  • Improving tests
  • Removing a blocker
  • Investigating uncertainty
  • Waiting briefly rather than starting unnecessary work

Keeping everyone individually occupied can increase work in progress and delay the Sprint Goal.

The Sprint Goal should guide the team's collective decisions.

A Sprint decision checklist

When deciding what to do next, ask:

  1. Which action most directly supports the Sprint Goal?
  2. What work is currently blocked?
  3. Can the blocker be removed collaboratively?
  4. Are too many items already in progress?
  5. Can the team finish something before starting more work?
  6. Has new information changed the plan?
  7. Is an interruption genuinely urgent?
  8. What scope will move if new work is accepted?
  9. Does the Sprint Goal remain achievable?
  10. Who needs to understand the change?
The Sprint Backlog is a living delivery plan, not a ticket queue assigned at Sprint Planning.
